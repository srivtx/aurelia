# 1. SKIN TYPE GUIDE

**What "skin type" actually means:** your skin type is mostly genetics — specifically how much sebum (oil) your skin naturally produces, plus how reactive it is. It can shift with seasons, hormones, age, and climate, so re-check yours a few times a year. **Sensitive** is a bit different: it can overlay any of the other four types (you can be "dry + sensitive" or "oily + sensitive"), which is why the quiz treats it as a flag as well as a type.

**How to read this section:** each type includes how to spot it, a 5-minute at-home test, a simple AM and PM routine (exact order), do's and don'ts, hero ingredients, and ingredients to skip.

## 1.1 NORMAL SKIN

**Snapshot:** balanced skin — not too oily, not too dry. Lucky, but still needs protection. (Note: "normal" just means "balanced" — every skin type is normal skin to have.)

### How to identify it
- **Shine pattern:** very little shine; maybe a slight glow on the forehead and nose by late afternoon.
- **Tightness after washing:** none — skin feels comfortable within an hour of cleansing.
- **Flaking:** rare, except maybe a patch in deep winter.
- **Pores:** small; visibly obvious only on and around the nose.
- **Redness & breakouts:** redness is rare; breakouts are occasional, small, and often show up around your period.
- **Makeup behavior:** sits well, doesn't slide off or cling to dry patches.

### 5-minute self-test
1. Wash your face with a gentle cleanser, pat dry, and apply **nothing**.
2. Wait **1 hour** (no touching, no phone leaning on your cheek).
3. Look in a mirror in natural light, then press a thin tissue over your face.
4. **Result:** no tightness, no all-over shine, tissue stays mostly clean → likely **Normal**. (Tiny shine on nose/forehead only = still Normal.)

### Morning routine (4 steps, in order)
1. **Rinse** with lukewarm water (or a gentle cleanser if you feel sweaty/greasy overnight).
2. **Lightweight hydrating toner or essence** (optional — nice hydration boost).
3. **Light lotion moisturizer.**
4. **Sunscreen SPF 30+** — always the last step.

### Evening routine (4 steps, in order)
1. **Gentle cleanser** (double cleanse — oil cleanser first, then gel — on days you wore makeup or SPF).
2. **Optional serum:** niacinamide (most nights) or vitamin C (alternate nights is fine).
3. **Moisturizer** — slightly richer than your AM one, still lightweight.
4. (Optional) **Lip balm** — your lips need care too.

### Do's ✅
- Do wear SPF every single day — Normal skin today is sun-damaged skin later if unprotected.
- Do keep the routine simple and consistent; boring and steady wins.
- Do moisturize even though your skin feels "fine" — prevention beats repair.
- Do re-check your skin type when seasons or hormones change.
- Do treat the occasional pimple gently (spot treat, don't squeeze).

### Don'ts ❌
- Don't over-exfoliate "for extra glow" — you'll trade Normal skin for irritation.
- Don't use harsh stripping products just because a routine "feels tingly."
- Don't pick at the rare pimple — that's how permanent dark marks start.
- Don't skip cleansing at night just because you're tired; 60 seconds counts.

### Hero ingredients
Hyaluronic acid · Niacinamide · Vitamin C (10% or a gentle derivative) · Peptides · Squalane · Lightweight ceramide lotion

### Ingredients to avoid (or deprioritize)
- Harsh sulfate (SLS-heavy) foaming cleansers — unneeded stripping.
- High-strength acids (10%+ AHA layers, frequent peels) — your skin doesn't need them.
- Products high in denatured alcohol ("SD alcohol") — drying with no benefit for you.
- Rough physical scrubs with jagged particles (crushed shells, apricot kernels).

## 1.2 OILY SKIN

**Snapshot:** your skin produces extra sebum all over. The upside nobody tells you: oily skin often ages more slowly and looks plumper later in life. The challenge is shine, bigger pores, and clogs.

### How to identify it
- **Shine pattern:** an all-over shiny/greasy look, often within an hour of washing — cheeks **and** forehead, not just the T-zone.
- **Tightness after washing:** rarely tight — more likely already shiny again soon after.
- **Pores:** visibly larger, especially on the nose, inner cheeks, and chin.
- **Texture:** blackheads and clogged pores are regular visitors; makeup slides off or breaks up by mid-afternoon.
- **Breakouts:** frequent, and often on the cheeks, jaw, chest, and back too.
- **Blotting test:** a tissue pressed on your face midday picks up oil from multiple zones.

### 5-minute self-test
1. Wash with a gentle cleanser, pat dry, apply **nothing**.
2. Wait **1 hour**.
3. Press a thin tissue or blotting paper on forehead, nose, **and** cheeks.
4. **Result:** oil picked up from all three areas (skin looks shiny in the mirror) → likely **Oily**. Shine strictly limited to forehead/nose/chin = Combination, not Oily.

### Morning routine (4–5 steps, in order)
1. **Gentle gel cleanser** (yes, wash in the morning — overnight oil + sweat).
2. **Hydrating toner/essence** (optional — hydration without heaviness).
3. **Niacinamide serum** (regulates oil look, supports barrier).
4. **Oil-free gel or fluid moisturizer** — yes, oily skin still needs it.
5. **Sunscreen SPF 30+** — gel, fluid, or "matte/oil-control" textures feel nicest.

### Evening routine (4–5 steps, in order)
1. **Oil cleanser or micellar water first** if you wore makeup and/or SPF (double cleanse).
2. **Gentle gel cleanser.**
3. **Salicylic acid (2%) serum 2–3 nights per week** (on the other nights, skip the acid).
4. **Gel moisturizer** (you can add the niacinamide serum step on non-acid nights).

### Do's ✅
- Do moisturize daily — dehydrated oily skin often pumps out *more* oil to compensate.
- Do use salicylic acid (BHA) 2–3 nights a week — it's oil-soluble, so it cleans inside pores.
- Do blot during the day instead of re-powdering cake layers.
- Do use a clay mask once a week if you like — one time, not daily.
- Do wear SPF — oily skin is not sun-proof, and sun damage thickens and darkens marks.

### Don'ts ❌
- Don't wash your face more than twice a day — over-washing triggers rebound oil.
- Don't skip moisturizer — it's the #1 oily-skin myth (see Section 7).
- Don't use alcohol-heavy "astringent" toners that sting — that tight feeling is damage, not clean.
- Don't scrub hard or use abrasive scrubs — irritation worsens breakouts.
- Don't pile on every acid at once — one active per routine is plenty.

### Hero ingredients
Niacinamide (5%) · Salicylic acid/BHA (2%) · Zinc · Lightweight gel moisturizers · Clay masks (1×/week) · Retinol or adapalene for breakouts (PM, see Section 4)

### Ingredients to avoid
- Heavy, rich creams and thick occlusive balms all over the face.
- Highly comedogenic oils used neat (e.g., unrefined coconut oil).
- Denatured alcohol-heavy toners and "cooling" menthol products.
- Over-drying clay masks used daily, and physical scrubs.

## 1.3 DRY SKIN

**Snapshot:** your skin produces less sebum, so it struggles to hold water. It craves ceramides, cream textures, and lukewarm water. Dry ≠ dehydrated: dry is a *type* (little oil), dehydrated is a *temporary state* (little water) that any type can have.

### How to identify it
- **Shine pattern:** almost none; skin looks matte, sometimes dull or papery.
- **Tightness after washing:** yes — tightness within minutes, sometimes flaking.
- **Flaking:** visible dry patches, especially around the nose, cheeks, and eyebrows in winter.
- **Pores:** small and tight.
- **Feel:** rough, tight, "parched"; moisturizer seems to vanish into it.
- **Lines:** fine dehydration lines show up earlier, especially under the eyes.

### 5-minute self-test
1. Wash with a gentle cleanser, pat dry, apply **nothing**.
2. Wait **1 hour** without touching your face.
3. Smile wide in the mirror and look at your cheeks.
4. **Result:** tightness, tiny flaky patches, or a dull crepey look when you smile → likely **Dry**. (Also try: press a tissue — no oil anywhere + tightness = Dry.)

### Morning routine (4–5 steps, in order)
1. **Rinse with lukewarm water only** or use a creamy/milky non-foaming cleanser.
2. **Hyaluronic acid serum on damp skin** (right after patting dry — damp skin is the trick).
3. **Cream moisturizer with ceramides.**
4. **Sunscreen SPF 30+** — moisturizing formulas exist; layer it after the cream.

### Evening routine (4–5 steps, in order)
1. **Cream/oil/balm cleanser** (melts SPF and makeup without stripping).
2. **Hyaluronic acid serum on damp skin.**
3. **Cream moisturizer (ceramides).**
4. **Optional: 2–3 drops of squalane or facial oil** as the final sealing step.

### Do's ✅
- Do apply hyaluronic acid to **damp** skin, then seal it with cream — order matters.
- Do use lukewarm (not hot) water for washing and showering.
- Do choose cream and balm textures over foaming gels, especially in winter.
- Do run a humidifier in dry/heated bedrooms overnight.
- Do give new products time — dry skin improves over weeks, not days.

### Don'ts ❌
- Don't take long hot showers or splash hot water on your face.
- Don't use foaming sulfate cleansers that leave a squeaky feel — "squeaky" = stripped.
- Don't exfoliate more than once a week (if at all).
- Don't use products with high denatured alcohol, strong fragrance, or menthol.
- Don't assume more flaking = "dead skin to scrub off" — usually it's a sign to *moisturize more, not scrub*.

### Hero ingredients
Ceramides · Hyaluronic acid · Glycerin · Squalane · Panthenol · Shea butter · Cream textures · Lactic acid (gentle weekly exfoliation) · Oat extract

### Ingredients to avoid
- SLS/sulfate foaming cleansers.
- High-strength glycolic acid peels and frequent acid layering.
- Denatured alcohol-heavy formulas, menthol, strong fragrance.
- Clay masks used often (they absorb the little oil you have).

## 1.4 COMBINATION SKIN

**Snapshot:** the most common type of all. Your T-zone (forehead, nose, chin) acts oily while your cheeks act normal or dry. Your superpower is *zone-treating*: different products (or amounts) on different areas.

### How to identify it
- **Shine pattern:** shiny forehead, nose, and chin by midday — cheeks stay matte or feel normal.
- **Tightness after washing:** cheeks may feel slightly tight; T-zone quickly gets shiny.
- **Pores:** visible on and around the nose, smaller on the cheeks.
- **Breakouts & blackheads:** concentrated in the T-zone.
- **Makeup behavior:** wears well on cheeks, slides on the nose by afternoon.
- **Seasons:** often more oily in summer, more tight-cheeked in winter.

### 5-minute self-test
1. Wash with a gentle cleanser, pat dry, apply **nothing**.
2. Wait **1 hour**.
3. Press separate tissues on your forehead/nose/chin, then each cheek.
4. **Result:** oil on the T-zone tissues, clean/dry cheek tissues → **Combination**. (Oily cheeks too = Oily; tight flaky cheeks + non-shiny T-zone = Dry.)

### Morning routine (4–5 steps, in order)
1. **Gentle gel cleanser** (one wash, whole face).
2. **Hydrating toner or essence** (optional, whole face).
3. **Niacinamide serum** — a hero for combination skin because it balances both zones.
4. **Lightweight lotion, with a little extra moisturizer patted onto cheeks.**
5. **Sunscreen SPF 30+.**

### Evening routine (4–5 steps, in order)
1. **Double cleanse** (oil cleanser first) if you wore SPF/makeup; otherwise one gentle cleanse.
2. **Salicylic acid 2–3 nights a week, applied mainly to the T-zone.**
3. **Niacinamide or hyaluronic acid serum on non-acid nights.**
4. **Gel-cream moisturizer on the T-zone + a richer cream on the cheeks** (two textures is normal and smart).

### Do's ✅
- Do treat zones differently — gel for T-zone, cream for cheeks.
- Do use BHA mainly where the clogs live (nose, chin, forehead).
- Do pick "gel-cream" hybrid textures if you want one product for everywhere.
- Do adjust with the seasons (lighter summer, richer winter).
- Do apply SPF every day, preferably a comfortable non-greasy finish.

### Don'ts ❌
- Don't treat your whole face as oily — your cheeks will pay for it with tightness and flaking.
- Don't skip cheek moisturizer because your nose is shiny.
- Don't use stripping all-over astringents.
- Don't apply rich cream thickly across your T-zone.

### Hero ingredients
Niacinamide (5%) · Hyaluronic acid · Salicylic acid (T-zone, 2–3×/week) · Gel-cream moisturizers · Ceramides (cheeks) · Vitamin C (alternate AMs)

### Ingredients to avoid
- Rich occlusive creams applied all over.
- Harsh foaming cleansers that upset the cheeks.
- Denatured alcohol-heavy toners.
- Physical scrubs (irritates both zones at once).

## 1.5 SENSITIVE SKIN

**Snapshot:** skin that reacts — stinging, redness, itching, or burning from products that most people tolerate. Can overlay any other type. Often it's actually a stressed *barrier*, which means the fix is usually "less, gentler, and fragrance-free" — not more products.

### How to identify it
- **Reaction pattern:** stinging or burning when you apply products (especially fragranced ones).
- **Redness:** flushes easily; visible redness around cheeks/nose; visible little capillaries.
- **After washing:** tightness *plus* warmth or pink patches.
- **Triggers:** new products, weather changes, sweat, rough fabrics, certain ingredients.
- **History:** you've had rashes, eczema patches, or reactions to products before.

### 5-minute self-test
1. Wash with a mild, fragrance-free cleanser, pat dry, apply nothing.
2. Wait **1 hour**.
3. Check your cheeks in the mirror and note how they *feel*.
4. **Result:** visible redness, heat, itching, or stinging → likely **Sensitive**. (Also note your base type: if you're also shiny, you're "oily + sensitive"; tight and flaky = "dry + sensitive.")

### Morning routine (4–5 steps, in order)
1. **Rinse with lukewarm water** or a milky, fragrance-free cleanser.
2. **Centella (cica) or panthenol soothing serum.**
3. **Fragrance-free barrier moisturizer with ceramides.**
4. **Mineral (zinc oxide/titanium dioxide) sunscreen SPF 30+** — mineral filters are usually the friendliest for reactive skin.

### Evening routine (4–5 steps, in order)
1. **Milky/cream fragrance-free cleanser** (one cleanse is often enough; use a soft cloth gently if removing SPF).
2. **Centella or aloe-based soothing serum.**
3. **Ceramide barrier cream.**
4. (Optional) **Spot-soothe** any red patches with a plain occlusive balm (e.g., plain petrolatum).

### Do's ✅
- Do choose **fragrance-free** products (check labels: "fragrance/parfum" — skip it).
- Do patch test every single new product (inner arm, 24–48h — full how-to in Section 8).
- Do introduce one new product at a time, at least 2 weeks apart, so you can identify the culprit.
- Do keep your routine short — 3–4 steps is genuinely best for you.
- Do write down what your skin reacts to; patterns emerge fast.

### Don'ts ❌
- Don't use products with fragrance, essential oils (including neat tea tree oil), or "botanical extracts" cocktails.
- Don't exfoliate with strong acids — if you must, try gentle azelaic acid or very occasional low-% lactic acid.
- Don't use physical scrubs, cleansing brushes, or rough towels on your face.
- Don't layer multiple actives in one routine.
- Don't keep using a product that stings, hoping you'll "get used to it" — stinging is a stop sign.

### Hero ingredients
Centella asiatica (cica) · Ceramides · Panthenol (B5) · Aloe vera (fragrance-free) · Oat extract · Azelaic acid (gentle option for redness + breakouts) · Mineral SPF filters · Squalane

### Ingredients to avoid
- Fragrance/parfum and essential oils.
- Denatured alcohol, witch hazel (alcohol-based), menthol, eucalyptus, camphor.
- High-% acids (10%+ AHA, daily BHA) and strong retinoids.
- Undiluted tea tree oil.

# 2. SKIN TYPE QUIZ DATA (interactive quiz spec)

**Format:** 10 multiple-choice questions. Each option awards points to one or more skin-type buckets: `Dry`, `Oily`, `Normal`, `Combination`, `Sensitive`. The app sums points per type and applies the result rules below.

## Scoring & result logic (implementation spec)

1. Sum points for each of the five types across all 10 questions.
2. **Combination override:** if `Oily ≥ 6` **and** `Dry ≥ 6` (i.e., strong signals for both), result = **Combination**.
3. Otherwise, result = the type with the **highest total**.
4. **Tie-break 1:** if tied, the winner is the type the user chose in **Q1** (how skin feels 1 hour after washing).
5. **Tie-break 2:** if still tied, priority order: Combination > Oily > Dry > Normal.
6. **Sensitive overlay:** if `Sensitive ≥ 4`, or the user selected **Q5 = A**, the result becomes "**[Base Type] + Sensitive**" (e.g., "Dry + Sensitive"), and the app should show the Sensitive skin-type guidance in addition to the base type.
7. Display reassurance line with every result: *"Skin types can shift with seasons, hormones, and age — retake this quiz in a few months, and remember there's no 'bad' type, just different care."*

**Max possible totals for calibration:** Oily 18, Dry 19, Normal 17, Combination 12, Sensitive 6. Sensitive tops out low by design — sensitivity co-exists with other types, so it's meant to win via the overlay rules (rule 6), not raw points. Combination also scores lower because rule 2 detects it (Oily ≥ 6 **and** Dry ≥ 6) even when neither individual column wins.

# 3. ROUTINE ARCHITECTURE 101

**The golden secret nobody markets:** a good routine is 3–5 steps, done daily. More steps ≠ better skin — consistency beats complexity, every time.

## What each step actually does

### 🧼 Cleanser
Removes sweat, excess oil, sunscreen, makeup, pollution, and dead cells so the rest of your routine can reach the skin. In the morning it clears overnight oil and sweat; at night it's essential — SPF and buildup left on overnight can clog pores. Use lukewarm water and a formula matched to your type (gel for oily, cream for dry, milky for sensitive).

### 💧 Toner (optional)
A modern hydrating toner or essence is a lightweight water-based layer that adds hydration and preps skin so serums absorb better — think of it as a damp base coat. Old-school "astringent" toners that sting and promise to shrink pores are best skipped (stinging = irritation, not cleaning). Toners with niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, or centella are gentle wins.

### 🧪 Serum
The "treatment" step — a thin, concentrated formula delivering an active ingredient to a specific goal (niacinamide for oil/pores, salicylic acid for clogs, vitamin C for brightness, hyaluronic acid for hydration). Serums go before moisturizer because their thin texture absorbs first; moisturizer then locks them in. One serum with one active per routine is plenty for beginners.

### 🧴 Moisturizer
Seals in hydration and repairs the skin barrier — the protective outer layer that keeps water in and irritants out. Even oily skin needs it (skin starved of moisture often produces *more* oil to compensate). Textures should match your type: gel or fluid for oily, lotion for combination, cream or balm for dry.

### ☀️ Sunscreen
The single most powerful step for long-term skin health — and the most skipped by teens. UV rays drive roughly 80% of visible facial aging (dark spots, texture, lines) and cause skin cancer; sunscreen is true prevention, not correction. It's always the **last step of your morning routine**, every day, indoors near windows included.

---

## The universal minimal routine (total beginners — start HERE)

**Morning:** cleanse (or just rinse, if dry/sensitive) → moisturize → **SPF 30+**
**Evening:** cleanse → moisturize
That's it. Run this for 4–6 weeks, consistently, before adding anything else. Your skin will thank you — and you'll learn your baseline. Add actives one at a time after that.

## Order rule: thinnest → thickest
Apply products in order of consistency, watery first, heavy last: **toner → serum (thinnest actives first) → lotion/cream → facial oil → SPF (last, morning only).** Reason: thin layers absorb quickly; a thick cream applied first would block everything behind it. Give each layer ~30–60 seconds to sink in before the next. SPF always goes last in the morning so it can form an even protective film.

## AM vs PM — what changes?
| | Morning | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Protect (hydration + SPF) | Repair (cleansing + actives + recovery) |
| **Cleansing** | Rinse or gentle cleanse | Full cleanse; double cleanse if you wore makeup/SPF |
| **Actives** | Antioxidants (vitamin C), niacinamide, hydration | Acids, retinol/adapalene, soothing serums |
| **Moisturizer** | Lighter texture | Slightly richer texture |
| **Sunscreen** | Always, last step | Never needed (no UV in bed) |
| **Extras** | — | Eye area gets extra care; treat breakouts |

**AM/PM split for actives (simple version):** vitamin C in the morning, retinol/acids at night, never two strong actives in the same routine.

# 4. INGREDIENT DICTIONARY (beginner-friendly)

**How to read a label:** ingredients are listed highest-to-lowest concentration (above ~1%). If a "hero" ingredient is near the end, it's marketing, not formulation. Notes: percentages are typical beginner-friendly ranges; "PM only" for acids/retinoids is about sun-sensitivity and gentleness, not danger.

## Hyaluronic Acid (HA)
- **What it does:** a humectant that pulls water into the skin and holds up to 1,000× its weight in water — instant hydration and a plumper look.
- **Best for:** every skin type, especially dehydrated skin of any type.
- **When:** AM and/or PM — apply to **damp** skin, then seal with moisturizer, or it can pull moisture back *out* of skin in dry air.
- **Beginner caution:** almost none; just don't use it as your only step without a moisturizer on top.

## Niacinamide (Vitamin B3)
- **What it does:** regulates oil production, strengthens the barrier, fades the look of dark marks, and softens the appearance of pores. One of the best-studied, most tolerant actives.
- **Best for:** all types — a hero for oily, combination, and breakout-prone skin.
- **When:** AM or PM (or both, at 4–5%).
- **Beginner caution:** very rare flushing at high strengths (10%+); 4–5% is plenty. Plays well with nearly everything.

## Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid & derivatives)
- **What it does:** a potent antioxidant that brightens, fades dark marks, boosts collagen support, and enhances your sunscreen's protection.
- **Best for:** normal, combination, oily; dullness and dark spots. (Sensitive types: pick gentle derivatives like SAP or THD ascorbate.)
- **When:** AM, under sunscreen (best value), or PM if skin prefers.
- **Beginner caution:** can tingle at first; start at 5–10% (or a derivative), store away from light/heat, and use it at a different time than retinol if you're new.

## Retinol (and the retinoid family)
- **What it does:** vitamin A derivatives that speed up cell turnover — the gold standard for clogged pores, breakouts, texture, and fine lines. Adapalene 0.1% is the drugstore-retinoid most recommended for teen acne.
- **Best for:** oily, combination, breakout-prone, texture concerns; dry skin with buffering; **not for pregnancy**.
- **When:** PM only, on dry skin (damp skin increases irritation).
- **Beginner caution:** start low (retinol ~0.1–0.3%) 2 nights a week and build up slowly; mild dryness/flaking is common, "purging" (small breakouts in clog-prone areas for 2–6 weeks) can happen before improvement. Daily SPF is non-negotiable while using it. Skip if pregnant or possibly pregnant.

## Salicylic Acid (BHA)
- **What it does:** the only common oil-soluble exfoliant — it dissolves into pores and clears out oil and dead cells. Ideal for blackheads, clogs, and oily skin.
- **Best for:** oily, combination, breakout-prone.
- **When:** PM, 2–3 nights a week to start.
- **Beginner caution:** mild tingling is normal; overuse causes flaking. One exfoliating acid per routine, not stacked with retinol on the same night.

## Glycolic Acid (AHA)
- **What it does:** a small, fast-penetrating water-soluble exfoliant that lifts dead surface cells — smoother texture, more glow, more even tone.
- **Best for:** normal, combination, mature; dullness and texture.
- **When:** PM, 1–2 nights a week to start.
- **Beginner caution:** stronger than it feels — stinging means it's working *too hard* for you; increases sun sensitivity (SPF daily). Skip while using retinol on the same night.

## Lactic Acid (AHA — the gentle one)
- **What it does:** a larger-molecule AHA that exfoliates the surface more gently while hydrating — smoothing flaky patches and softening texture.
- **Best for:** dry, sensitive (introducing acids slowly), and beginners.
- **When:** PM, 1 night a week to start.
- **Beginner caution:** gentler than glycolic but still sun-sensitizing; don't layer with retinol the same night.

## Azelaic Acid
- **What it does:** calms redness, unclogs pores, and gently fades dark marks — a multitasker that's unusually kind to reactive skin.
- **Best for:** sensitive, rosacea-prone, acne + redness combo, post-acne marks.
- **When:** AM or PM.
- **Beginner caution:** slight tingling the first week is common; 10% strengths are typically well tolerated.

## Ceramides
- **What it does:** skin-identical lipids ("mortar" between your skin-cell "bricks") that repair and reinforce the barrier so moisture stays in and irritants stay out.
- **Best for:** everyone — essential for dry and sensitive skin.
- **When:** AM and PM, inside your moisturizer.
- **Beginner caution:** none — pure support, no downside.

## Peptides
- **What it does:** short amino-acid chains that signal skin to maintain collagen — a gentle firming/moisturizing support act for the long game.
- **Best for:** all types; nice for early fine lines.
- **When:** AM or PM, under moisturizer.
- **Beginner caution:** none meaningful; expectations should be subtle, not dramatic.

## Panthenol (Pro-Vitamin B5)
- **What it does:** soothes, hydrates, and supports barrier repair — the classic "calm down" ingredient.
- **Best for:** all types, especially sensitive or irritated skin.
- **When:** AM/PM.
- **Beginner caution:** none — very well tolerated.

## Zinc (PCA / Zinc salts in formulas)
- **What it does:** mild oil-regulating and calming mineral support; also the star of mineral sunscreens (zinc oxide) as a broad-spectrum UV filter.
- **Best for:** oily, sensitive; mineral SPF for reactive skin.
- **When:** any time, within formulas.
- **Beginner caution:** topical zinc is gentle — but avoid harsh "drying lotion" zinc formulas that sting.

## Aloe Vera
- **What it does:** a classic soothing, cooling, hydrating plant extract for tight, sun-warmed, or irritated skin.
- **Best for:** sensitive, oily (light gel hydration), sun-stressed skin.
- **When:** AM/PM.
- **Beginner caution:** check the label — many aloe gels sneak in denatured alcohol and fragrance, which cancel out the soothing.

## Centella Asiatica (Cica)
- **What it does:** a calming herb that reduces redness and supports the skin's own repair processes — the backbone of "cica" products for reactive skin.
- **Best for:** sensitive, redness-prone, barrier-damaged skin; honestly, anyone.
- **When:** AM/PM.
- **Beginner caution:** none — famously well tolerated.

## Squalane
- **What it does:** a lightweight, skin-identical oil that seals in moisture and softens without clogging pores for most people.
- **Best for:** dry skin (the final PM step), all types that want extra comfort.
- **When:** PM, last step before (or instead of) richer cream.
- **Beginner caution:** very low; oily skin may prefer it sparingly.

## SPF Filters (Mineral vs Chemical)
- **What it does:** UV filters. **Chemical/organic** (avobenzone, octisalate, etc.) absorb UV and rub in invisibly; **mineral/inorganic** (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) sit on top and reflect/absorb UV.
- **Best for:** chemical for elegant everyday textures; mineral for sensitive/reactive skin and eyes that sting.
- **When:** AM, last step, every day.
- **Beginner caution:** mineral can leave a white cast on deeper skin tones — tinted versions help; chemical filters can sting eyes/sensitive skin. Any SPF you'll happily wear daily is the right one.

## ⚠️ Ingredients NOT to mix (in the same routine)
| Combo | Why to skip | Beginner workaround |
|---|---|---|
| **Retinol + Vitamin C** | Different pH sweet spots + compounding irritation risk for new skin | Vitamin C in AM · retinol in PM |
| **Retinol + AHA/BHA (same night)** | Double turnover = peeling, redness, barrier damage | Retinol Mon/Wed, acid Tue/Thu |
| **Retinol + Benzoyl peroxide** | BPO can deactivate classic retinol (adapalene is the stable exception) | Use adapalene with BPO, or AM/PM split |
| **High-% Vitamin C + direct acids** | Stability drops, stinging rises | Different days, or a vitamin C derivative |
| **Two strong acids at once** | Over-exfoliation city | One exfoliant, 1–3 nights/week |

## ✅ Safe combos (stack freely)
- **Niacinamide + literally almost anything** — even improves retinol tolerance.
- **Hyaluronic acid + everything** — hydration layer for any routine.
- **Ceramides/panthenol/squalane + any active** — they buffer and support.
- **Vitamin C + SPF** in the morning — antioxidant + protection team up.
- **Azelaic acid + most gentle actives** — unusually compatible.
- **Retinol + plain moisturizer** — the "sandwich method" (moisturize → retinol → moisturize) for sensitive beginners.

**The 60-second rule of thumb:** when in doubt, one *active* per routine, actives split AM/PM, and hydrating/barrier layers stacked as desired.

# 5. COMMON SKIN CONCERNS QUICK GUIDE

Friendly framing for app display: *"Concerns are normal — every single one of these is extremely common at 16–25. Solutions work gradually: give any consistent routine 8–12 weeks before judging it."*

## 1) Acne / Pimples
- **Cause:** pores get plugged with oil + dead cells; bacteria (C. acnes) thrive in the plug; hormones (periods, puberty, stress) rev up the oil. It is **not** a dirt or hygiene problem.
- **Gentle solutions:** (1) 2% salicylic acid cleanser or serum 2–3×/week; (2) benzoyl peroxide 2.5% as a spot treatment (2.5% works nearly as well as 10% with far less irritation); (3) adapalene 0.1% (PM) — the first-line OTC retinoid for teen acne. Never pick — that converts a 5-day pimple into a 3-month dark mark.
- **See a dermatologist when:** pimples are deep, painful, or cystic; acne is scarring; OTC routines show no change after 6–8 weeks; or it's affecting your confidence (a totally valid reason — prescription options exist and work well).

## 2) Blackheads
- **Cause:** an open, oil-filled pore whose contents oxidize and turn dark when it meets air. Not dirt, not "unclean skin."
- **Gentle solutions:** (1) oil-soluble salicylic acid 2–3×/week; (2) a gentle retinoid (adapalene) to keep pores clear long-term; (3) forget squeezing and ripping pore strips — both enlarge the pore's appearance over time.
- **See a dermatologist when:** blackheads are widespread and unresponsive after ~3 months — professional extraction exists, done safely.

## 3) Dark Spots / Hyperpigmentation (post-acne marks)
- **Cause:** after inflammation (a pimple, a picked scab), skin overproduces melanin in that spot — "post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation" (PIH). Sun exposure deepens it. Extra note for deeper skin tones: PIH is more common and lasts longer — and picking is the main amplifier.
- **Gentle solutions:** (1) daily SPF 30+ — the single most effective step, since UV re-darkens every spot; (2) azelaic acid, niacinamide, or vitamin C to fade marks over months; (3) stop the picking — prevention of new marks. Fading takes 2–6+ months; anyone promising "gone in a week" is selling something.
- **See a dermatologist when:** dark patches appear symmetrically on cheeks/forehead without injury (could be melasma — needs different care), or spots change shape/color/border (always worth a check).

## 4) Dullness
- **Cause:** a buildup of dead surface cells, dehydration, poor sleep, or no SPF (UV slowly roughens skin texture). It's a state, not a type — and it's fixable.
- **Gentle solutions:** (1) gentle exfoliation 1–2×/week (lactic or glycolic); (2) daily hydration — HA serum + moisturizer on damp skin; (3) the boring trio: sleep, water, SPF. Vitamin C (AM) adds brightness.
- **See a dermatologist when:** dullness comes with fatigue, hair loss, or other body symptoms (thyroid/anemia are common and treatable).

## 5) Enlarged Pores
- **Cause:** mostly genetics + how much oil your pores handle; oil and loss of skin firmness make them more visible. Pores **cannot** be permanently "shrunk" or "opened/closed" — they aren't doors.
- **Gentle solutions:** (1) salicylic acid keeps pores empty = visually smaller; (2) niacinamide 5% daily refines their look over weeks; (3) retinoids + SPF maintain the collagen that keeps pores tight-looking long-term.
- **See a dermatologist when:** texture changes are severe or sudden; professional treatments exist but are rarely needed under 25.

## 6) Dark Circles
- **Cause:** often genetics (thin under-eye skin showing underlying muscle/vessels), plus sleep debt, allergies ("allergic shiners"), dehydration, and rubbing your eyes. Not always "tiredness," and not always fixable with cream.
- **Gentle solutions:** (1) consistent sleep (7–9 hours) + treating allergies; (2) SPF and sunglasses — UV thins and darkens that delicate skin; (3) cool compress + a caffeine or vitamin C eye serum for a modest temporary brightening. Be gentle: never rub the eye area.
- **See a dermatologist when:** circles appear suddenly, one-sided, with swelling, or with other symptoms — and keep expectations honest: some circles are anatomy, and that's fine.

## 7) Texture / Bumpy Skin
- **Cause:** micro-clogs in pores, or keratosis pilaris (tiny keratin bumps, usually on upper arms/cheeks — genetic and extremely common in teens, often fading with age).
- **Gentle solutions:** (1) a gentle chemical exfoliant 1–2×/week (lactic acid is ideal for KP); (2) a retinoid at night for clog-related texture; (3) consistent moisturizing — hydrated skin is smoother skin. Avoid scrubs: they micro-tear and worsen bumps.
- **See a dermatologist when:** bumps are painful, spreading, or KP is severe — stronger smoothing options are available.

# 6. SUNSCREEN GUIDE (the most important 30 seconds of your day)

## Why SPF matters daily
- Research attributes roughly **80% of visible facial aging** — dark spots, uneven tone, fine lines, sagging — to UV exposure (Flament et al., 2013). Starting at 16 rather than 30 is a genuine advantage: you're preventing, not correcting.
- UV rays are present **year-round**, on cloudy days (up to ~80% of UV still gets through cloud cover), and in winter.
- Sunscreen also prevents skin cancer — the most common cancer in young people — and keeps dark spots (Section 5.3) from getting darker.

## UVA vs UVB (know your enemy)
- **UVB** = the "burning" rays — strongest 10am–4pm, mostly blocked by window glass.
- **UVA** = the "aging" rays — steady all day, all year, **passes through window glass and clouds**, penetrates deeper into skin. This is why "indoor" and "cloudy" don't mean "safe."
- Choose **"broad spectrum"** so you're covered against both.

## SPF 30 vs SPF 50 — what the numbers mean
- SPF is a multiplier of how long you can stay in the sun before burning, but the clearer way to read it is **% of UVB filtered:**
  - SPF 15 ≈ 93% filtered
  - SPF 30 ≈ 97% filtered
  - SPF 50 ≈ 98% filtered
- The jump from 30 to 50 is real but small — **and the amount you apply matters far more than the number.** Most people apply a quarter of the tested amount, turning "SPF 50" into "SPF ~10." An SPF 30 you apply properly beats an SPF 50 you apply thinly. (SPF above 50 adds little.)

## How much to apply — the two-finger rule
- Squeeze sunscreen in **two lines, from the base to the tip of your index and middle fingers** — that's the right amount for your **face + neck** (Taylor & Diffey, 2002; ≈ ¼ teaspoon, the tested 2 mg/cm² dose).
- Application tips: apply as the **last morning step** (after moisturizer), wait ~5 minutes before makeup, don't forget ears, hairline, and the front/back of the neck.

## Reapplication
- **Every 2 hours** of continuous sun exposure, and **after swimming, sweating, or toweling off.**
- In daily indoor life (school, office), one solid morning application is reasonable; reapply before going outside at lunch or for PE/sports.
- Over makeup: sunscreen sticks, sprays, or patting an SPF cushion/powder over the top all work.

## The indoor/window myth — busted
- **"I'm indoors all day, I don't need SPF."** Partly true, mostly false: UVA passes through standard window glass. Working or studying beside a sunny window, long car/bus rides, and plane windows = real UVA exposure. UVB (the burning kind) is largely blocked by glass, which is exactly why people don't feel the damage happening. If you sit near a window, SPF still earns its place.
- Screens (blue light): evidence is limited and mixed — don't stress about your phone; do stress about the sun.

# 7. SKINCARE MYTHS — DEBUNKED

| # | The myth | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Oily skin doesn't need moisturizer."** | FALSE — dehydrated skin often compensates by producing *more* oil. A light gel moisturizer usually means *less* shine, not more. |
| 2 | **"Natural is always better / chemicals are bad."** | FALSE — poison ivy is natural; water is a chemical. Well-tested "lab" ingredients (ceramides, HA) are often gentler than raw lemon or essential oils. Judge the formula, not the vibe. |
| 3 | **"Toothpaste dries out pimples."** | FALSE — toothpaste is formulated for teeth, not skin: it can burn the spot, cause irritation, and leave a dark mark that outlives the pimple by months. Use benzoyl peroxide instead. |
| 4 | **"Higher SPF = way more protection."** | MOSTLY FALSE — SPF 30 blocks ~97% of UVB and SPF 50 ~98%. A 2% difference is negligible; applying *enough* matters far more than the number. |
| 5 | **"You only need sunscreen when it's sunny."** | FALSE — up to ~80% of UV penetrates clouds, and UVA comes through windows. Daily SPF is the rule. |
| 6 | **"Hot water and cold water open/close your pores."** | FALSE — pores have no muscles and no doors. Warm water softens the oil *inside* pores (helpful for cleansing), but pore size is genetics + oil. |
| 7 | **"Exfoliating daily gives faster results."** | FALSE — skin renews on ~28-day cycles in your teens/20s. Daily scrubbing damages the barrier and causes redness, flaking, and *more* breakouts. 1–3×/week maximum. |
| 8 | **"Acne means your face is dirty."** | FALSE — acne is oil + hormones + genetics + bacteria, not dirt. Over-washing strips the barrier and can make acne worse. Twice daily gentle cleansing is plenty. |
| 9 | **"You'll see results in a few days."** | FALSE — skin cell turnover takes ~28 days, so realistic timelines are: hydration ~2 weeks, texture ~4–8 weeks, dark spots 2–6+ months. Consistency > intensity. |
| 10 | **"Tanning clears up acne."** | FALSE + risky — a tan may temporarily redden/camouflage skin, but UV increases post-acne dark marks, ages skin, and raises skin-cancer risk. Tanning beds are never a treatment. |
| 11 | **"The more products, the better the skin."** | FALSE — a consistent 3–5 step routine beats a 12-step shelf. Every added product is added irritation risk. |
| 12 | **"Chocolate and pizza cause breakouts."** | MOSTLY MYTH — strong direct links are unproven; evidence points weakly to high-glycemic diets and (for some people) lots of skim dairy. Eat balanced, but no single food is a universal acne trigger — your skin is not ruined by one slice. |

# 8. HABITS & LIFESTYLE (the free 40% of good skin)

## Pillowcase — your face spends 8 hours a night on it
- Change/wash your **pillowcase 1–2× per week** — it collects oil, dead cells, and bacteria that get pressed back into your skin nightly.
- Breakout-prone: consider flipping the pillowcase midweek, and rinse conditioner/product residue off your hairline before bed (haircare buildup on the hairline causes clogged "acne cosmetica" breakouts).

## Hands off your face
- Your hands touch phones, desks, and doorknobs — then your chin. **Touching and resting your face transfers oil and bacteria** and fuels breakouts.
- **Your phone screen** is a top offender for cheek breakouts: wipe it daily, and use earphones on long calls instead of pressing it to your cheek.
- **Never pick or pop.** Squeezing pushes inflammation deeper, raises scarring risk, and turns a small pimple into a long-lasting dark mark. If you must treat a pimple: spot treatment + a hydrocolloid patch overnight.

## Water, sleep, diet — the honest version
- **Water:** hydration supports overall health, and dehydrated skin looks duller — but drinking extra gallons doesn't overwrite skin type. Aim for steady daily hydration, not magic numbers.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours is real "beauty sleep" — skin does its repair work overnight, and sleep loss raises cortisol (more oil, more breakouts, duller tone).
- **Diet:** no food is universally banned. If anything, evidence weakly favors lower-glycemic eating and watching heavy skim-dairy intake for acne-prone people. Balanced meals, colorful veggies, and not stressing over one "bad" meal is the goal.
- **Never smoke/vape:** beyond everything else, it's one of the fastest accelerators of premature skin aging.

## Patch-testing new products — the 24–48 hour rule
New product? Run a tiny experiment before your whole face tries it:
1. Apply a small amount (about the size of a pea, or a quarter-sized patch) to your **inner arm**, or the **side of your neck / behind your ear**.
2. Leave it on, don't wash it off, and **wait 24–48 hours** — delayed reactions can appear up to 72–96 hours later.
3. **Any redness, itching, burning, or bumps = stop; that product is not for you.** No reaction = cautiously good to try on your face.
4. For sensitive/reactive skin or leave-on actives (retinoids, acids), the American Academy of Dermatology recommends testing the same spot **twice a day for 7–10 days** before committing.
5. **Golden rule:** introduce only **one new product at a time**, at least 1–2 weeks apart — otherwise you can never tell which product caused a reaction (or a win).

## Small habits with outsized payoffs
- **Take off makeup + SPF before sleep** — 60 seconds with a cleansing balm or oil. Overnight makeup clogs pores and ages the eye area.
- **Clean makeup brushes and sponges weekly** (mild soap + lukewarm water, dry flat) and never share makeup or tools with friends.
- **Lukewarm water on your face, always** — hot water feels nice and strips skin.
- **Rinse or wash your face after sweaty workouts** so sweat doesn't sit and marinate.
- **Manage stress** however works for you — exam-season breakouts are a cortisol story, not a you-failed story. Be kind to your skin and to yourself. 💙
